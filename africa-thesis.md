# The Africa Thesis

*This is the core argument behind Kaguura Gichuru's essay "The Most Exciting Startup Scene In The World Is Building In The Dark," published on The Write Path.*

## The Argument

African fintech dominance is not a triumph — it is a misalignment. The continent's unicorns are overwhelmingly concentrated in financial services, while the actual infrastructure that would make those financial services meaningful remains unbuilt.

The wealthiest Africans did not get wealthy through fintech. They got wealthy through energy, real estate, construction, retail, and agriculture. The startup ecosystem is building the application layer on top of an infrastructure layer that does not yet exist.

The comparison that matters: post-WWII America was not in a consumer tech moment. It was in an infrastructure moment — highways, energy grids, universities, industrial capacity. The consumer technology era followed because the infrastructure was there to support it. Africa is in that earlier moment right now.

## The Data Point

Africa's fintech concentration is the most extreme of any major emerging market globally. Latin America skews heavily fintech but shows more sectoral diversity. Africa's startup funding is more concentrated in a single sector than any comparable region.

## The Prescription

The arbitrage is in the layers below the application: energy, logistics, industrial capacity, education infrastructure. The founders building there are not the ones getting profiled in TechCrunch. They are the ones who will be worth studying in 20 years.

## Why It Matters

Africa's next two decades will be the most significant wealth creation event in modern history — driven by demographics, urbanization, and the compounding effect of being early to infrastructure buildout. The question is whether the startup ecosystem is aligned with that moment or performing a different era's playbook.
