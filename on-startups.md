# On Building

*Kaguura Gichuru's working philosophy on startups, leverage, and building from Africa.*

## Two Engines

There are two ways to compound.

The first is words and thinking — writing, publishing, building an audience. This engine builds authority first and money second. It is slow at the start and then, suddenly, it is not.

The second is code and products — software, systems, things that work while you sleep. This engine builds money first and authority second.

The best position is to run both simultaneously, understanding that they feed each other. An audience makes distribution cheaper. Revenue makes the writing more credible. Treating them as alternatives is the mistake.

## On Time

Time is the only resource you cannot recover. Giving it away to fulfil someone else's dream, indefinitely, at the expense of your own, is the one trade that cannot be undone. This is not a philosophical position. It is the foundational logic behind every business decision.

## On Wealth

Wealth is not purely financial. It compounds in the way that Proverbs 13 describes: to those who have, more will be given. Accumulating wealth is not a moral failing — it is a tool. The question is what you do with it. The goal is not to be rich. The goal is to be wealthy enough to make a meaningful dent. That requires getting serious about the accumulation first.

## On Building in Africa

Western startup playbooks were written for Western infrastructure. Applying them to African markets without modification is not ambition — it is cargo cult thinking. The opportunity in Africa is in reading the actual moment, not the imported one. The actual moment is infrastructure. The arbitrage is early.

## On Selection

Work has to be worth doing. Not just financially — worth doing in the sense that you would be proud of it in ten years. The organic diaper company took nine months. It was worth it. The question before taking any engagement: would I do this myself?
